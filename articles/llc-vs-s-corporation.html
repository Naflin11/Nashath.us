LLC vs S Corporation: Which May Be Right for Your Business?

Understand the main differences in taxation, payroll, administration and owner responsibilities.

Published July 23, 2026 • 5-minute read

LLC and S Corporation are two terms commonly used when discussing small-business taxes. However, they do not describe exactly the same thing.

An LLC is a legal business structure created under state law. An S Corporation is a federal tax classification available to eligible businesses. This means an LLC may remain an LLC legally while electing S Corporation tax treatment.

Key point: An LLC describes the legal structure of a business, while an S Corporation mainly describes how an eligible business is taxed.

What Is an LLC?

A Limited Liability Company, or LLC, is a business structure established under state law. Its owners are called members, and the business may have one or multiple owners.

An LLC may help separate certain business liabilities from the owners' personal assets. However, owners must still operate the company properly, maintain accurate records and keep business and personal finances separate.

For federal tax purposes, a single-member LLC is generally treated as part of the owner's tax return. A multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership unless another tax election is made.

What Is an S Corporation?

An S Corporation is a federal tax status that an eligible business may elect. A qualifying LLC can elect this treatment without changing its legal structure.

The business generally files Form 2553 to request S Corporation status. Once approved, it normally files Form 1120-S each year, and the business income passes through to its shareholders.

LLC vs S Corporation: Main Differences

Area Default LLC Taxation S Corporation Taxation
Legal structure LLC May still be an LLC
Tax treatment Usually sole proprietor or partnership S Corporation
Owner payments Usually owner draws Payroll wages and possible distributions
Payroll Usually not required for the owner Generally required for working shareholders
Administration Usually simpler Usually more complex

Tax and Payroll Considerations

Under default LLC taxation, qualifying business earnings may generally be subject to self-employment tax in addition to income tax.

With S Corporation treatment, an owner who works in the business generally must receive a reasonable salary through payroll. Remaining eligible profits may sometimes be distributed separately.

This does not mean an S Corporation automatically saves money. The potential tax difference should be compared with:

  • Payroll processing expenses
  • Bookkeeping costs
  • Tax preparation fees
  • Federal and state filing requirements
  • Reasonable compensation requirements
A working shareholder generally should not take all business earnings as distributions while avoiding a reasonable salary.

Which Option May Be Suitable?

Default LLC taxation may be suitable when:

  • The business is new
  • Profit is limited or inconsistent
  • The owner prefers simpler administration
  • The business is not ready to operate payroll
  • The potential S Corporation benefit is small

An S Corporation election may be considered when:

  • The business earns consistent profit
  • The owner actively works in the company
  • The business can support a reasonable salary
  • Bookkeeping records are accurate
  • The possible benefit justifies the additional cost and administration

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming an LLC and an S Corporation are completely separate business structures
  • Making an S election without calculating payroll and compliance costs
  • Paying an unreasonably low owner salary
  • Missing the Form 2553 filing deadline
  • Mixing personal and business transactions
  • Assuming an S Corporation always reduces taxes

Making the Right Decision

There is no single option that is best for every business. The decision should consider expected profit, owner salary, payroll costs, bookkeeping, state taxes and future growth plans.

A personalized comparison can help determine whether remaining under default LLC taxation or electing S Corporation treatment may be more appropriate.

Frequently Asked Questions

LLC and S Corporation FAQs

Is an LLC automatically an S Corporation?

No. An LLC receives its default federal tax treatment unless it makes an eligible S Corporation election.

Can a single-member LLC elect S Corporation status?

Yes, a qualifying single-member LLC may elect S Corporation treatment if it meets the eligibility and filing requirements.

Does an S Corporation owner need payroll?

A shareholder who performs services for the business generally must receive reasonable compensation through payroll.

Does an S Corporation always save taxes?

No. The result depends on business profit, owner salary, payroll costs, state obligations and other circumstances.
